EJU maths looks like multiple choice, but it isn't, really. Most questions give you a row of lettered boxes (A, B, C and so on), and you fill in your answer **one character per box**. So you're not picking from four options. You're building the number yourself.

This catches a lot of students on their first mock. They solve the problem correctly and still lose the marks, because the answer doesn't fit the boxes the way JASSO expects.

## The five rules

**1. One box, one mark.** Each box is a single digit from 0 to 9, or a minus sign. If the answer is −3 and there are two boxes, the first is "−" and the second is "3".

**2. Signs go on top.** A negative fraction carries its minus sign on the numerator. So $-\frac{2}{5}$ is written with "−2" in the top boxes and "5" in the bottom box. Every fraction must be fully reduced.

**3. Keep roots small.** Leave the smallest possible whole number inside a root. Write $4\sqrt{2}$, never $2\sqrt{8}$. If your answer doesn't fit the boxes, simplify again.

**4. Mark your course.** Mark Course 1 or Course 2 on the maths sheet **and** circle it. If you forget, the paper isn't scored at all. This is the most expensive mistake on the whole exam.

**5. Mark clearly.** Faint marks aren't read. Rub out mistakes completely with a plastic eraser.

## A useful habit

The boxes are a hint. If the question gives you $\frac{\boxed{A}\sqrt{\boxed{B}}}{\boxed{C}}$ and your answer is $\frac{\sqrt{12}}{3}$, you know immediately something isn't simplified, because there's a box for a number in front of the root. Simplify to $\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$ and it fits.

Use the box shape to check your answer before you mark it.
