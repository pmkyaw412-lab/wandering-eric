JASSO publishes **twenty past EJU sittings, from 2010 to 2021, for free, with answers**. They're the closest thing you'll get to the real exam, and a lot of students either don't know about them or burn through them too early.

You can view and print them for your own study. JASSO doesn't allow them to be re-posted, so I link to JASSO's own pages rather than hosting copies. The full list, with a direct link to each sitting, is in [Study → EJU practice](#/study/eju/papers).

## What's included

The older sittings cover all subjects. The four most recent ones include only science and maths, without Japanese or Japan and the World. Plan your Japanese practice with that in mind.

## How I'd use them

Twenty papers sounds like a lot, but it goes quickly if you use them as homework. A simple plan:

1. **Learn first, test later.** Don't start past papers before you've covered the syllabus. Use [the formula list](#/study) and your textbook first. A past paper is a test, not a lesson.
2. **Keep the newest ones for the end.** Recent papers are closest to the current style. Save four or five for the last two months before your exam.
3. **Always time yourself.** Do each paper in one sitting, at real exam length. Time pressure is half of what makes the EJU hard.
4. **Use the real answer format.** Fill in boxes exactly as on the day. If you haven't yet, read [how the maths answer sheet works](#/blog/eju-maths-answer-sheet).
5. **Mark, then redo.** After marking, redo every question you got wrong without looking at the solution. Add the formulas you forgot to your [review list](#/study).

## One honest note

Past papers show you the style and the level. They won't teach you a topic you skipped. If you keep losing marks on the same topic, go back to the formula and practise it properly before the next paper.
