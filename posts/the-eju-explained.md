The EJU (日本留学試験, Examination for Japanese University Admission for International Students) is the test most Japanese universities use to admit international students. It's run by JASSO twice a year, in June and November, in Japan and in a few other countries.

Here's what you actually need to know before you register.

## When it happens

There are two sittings a year. Applications close about **three months before** each exam, which surprises a lot of students. For the November 2026 sitting, applications ran from 6 to 30 July, and the exam is on 8 November. Results come out on EJU Online, planned for 15 December 2026.

The live dates, with how many days are left, are always in [Study → EJU practice](#/study/eju/dates).

## The four subjects

You only sit the subjects your universities ask for. Nobody takes all four.

- **Japanese as a Foreign Language (日本語):** reading, listening and listening-reading, scored together out of 400. The writing task (記述) is scored separately out of 50. Japanese only.
- **Mathematics (数学):** out of 200. Course 1 covers 数学I and 数学A. Course 2 covers 数学I, II, III, A, B and C.
- **Science (理科):** two of physics, chemistry and biology, out of 100 each. Your faculty decides which two.
- **Japan and the World (総合科目):** politics, economics, geography and history, out of 200.

Every subject except Japanese can be taken in Japanese or English. You choose the language when you apply, so check which one your universities accept first.

## Rules that catch people out

1. **Science or Japan and the World, never both.** You can't sit both in the same sitting, so decide your direction early.
2. **You choose the maths course on the answer sheet, not when you register.** You register for "Mathematics". On the day you mark Course 1 or Course 2 on the sheet and circle it. If it isn't marked properly, the paper isn't scored. I've written a separate post on [how the maths answer sheet works](#/blog/eju-maths-answer-sheet).
3. **The 募集要項 is the rule.** Each university's application guidelines say exactly which subjects, which course and which language it accepts. Read it before you register, not after.

## What faculties usually ask for

This is the typical pattern at national universities. Many private universities ask for fewer subjects, sometimes only Japanese.

| Field | Usually asked for |
|---|---|
| Engineering, information science | Japanese, Maths Course 2, Science (usually physics and chemistry) |
| Science | Japanese, Maths Course 2, Science (the two closest to your course) |
| Medicine, dentistry, pharmacy | Japanese, Maths Course 2, Science (chemistry with biology or physics) |
| Agriculture | Japanese, Maths Course 2, Science (usually chemistry and biology) |
| Nursing and health | Japanese and Science, often with maths; varies a lot |
| Economics and business | Japanese, Japan and the World, Maths Course 1 (some economics faculties ask for Course 2) |
| Law, humanities, social sciences | Japanese, Japan and the World, often Maths Course 1 |
| Education | Japanese, then Japan and the World or Science depending on the course |
| Art and music | Japanese, often with their own portfolio, audition or practical test |

If you're aiming for science or engineering, plan for Course 2 from the start. Switching late is painful.

## How the score works

EJU scores are **equated**. That means they're placed on a common scale, so the same score means the same level in June and in November, even if one paper was harder. Your score is **not** the percentage of questions you got right. Only the Japanese writing task is marked directly.

There's no pass mark. Each university decides what it needs. A score can be used for up to two years, but each university chooses which sittings it accepts, so check that too.
