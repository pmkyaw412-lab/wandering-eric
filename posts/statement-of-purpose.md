The 志望理由書 is where most applicants lose the chance to stand out. Not because they write badly, but because they write the same thing as everyone else.

A structure that works well is **five short parts, in this order**. Write in the language your university asks for. If it's Japanese, length is counted in characters (字), so check the limit.

You can draft each part separately in [Study → Application → Statement](#/study/apply/statement). It shows one part at a time and counts characters as you type.

## 1. What started this

The specific moment or problem that made you want this subject. **One event, not a life story.**

Avoid "since I was a child". Almost every applicant writes it, and it tells the reader nothing.

## 2. What you've done since

Study, projects, work, reading. This is your evidence that the interest survived actual effort.

Name what you actually did. "I studied hard" isn't evidence. "I worked through every past EJU physics paper and kept a notebook of the mistakes I repeated" is.

## 3. Why Japan

Something about this subject that's better done in Japan than at home: a technology, an industry, a research tradition, a kind of training.

Culture, anime and safety are fine reasons to enjoy living here. They aren't reasons to study engineering here.

## 4. Why this university, this faculty

A named lab, professor, course or facility. Read their website until you find one that genuinely connects to what you wrote in parts 1 and 2.

This is the part admissions staff use to separate serious applicants from people sending the same letter to twenty universities. Spend the most time here.

## 5. What you'll do with it

Where this degree takes you, as concretely as you honestly can. It doesn't need to be a ten-year plan. It needs to follow logically from parts 1 to 4.

## Before you send it

- Read it aloud. If a sentence is hard to say, it's hard to read.
- Check every name: the university, the faculty, the professor.
- If it's in Japanese, ask a teacher to check it. Small grammar errors matter less than unclear meaning, but both are worth fixing.
- Check whether it must be handwritten.

Many universities follow up with an interview, and the questions often come straight from your statement. See [interview and essay practice](#/blog/interview-and-essay).
