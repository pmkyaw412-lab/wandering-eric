After documents and the EJU, many universities add an interview, an essay (小論文), or both. They feel unpredictable, but they're more predictable than they look.

## The interview

Most interview questions fall into a small set: why this subject, why Japan, why this university, what you've studied, what you'll do after, and a few about life in Japan. Many come straight from your [statement of purpose](#/blog/statement-of-purpose), so read it again the night before.

How to practise:

1. **Answer out loud, in Japanese.** Thinking an answer and saying it are different skills. Say it.
2. **Time yourself.** Most answers should take **under a minute**. Long answers usually mean you're not sure what your point is.
3. **Start with the answer.** Give your main point in the first sentence, then the reason, then one example.
4. **Prepare for "anything else?"** Have one question ready about the faculty or a course. It shows you've read about them.

There's a question bank in [Study → Application → Interview](#/study/apply/interview), with real-style questions in Japanese first, the English underneath, a tip for each, and a timer.

## The essay (小論文)

Most 小論文 questions come in one of three shapes. Recognising the shape in the first minute tells you how to plan.

**Opinion (意見型):** 「〜について、あなたの考えを述べなさい」

- 序論: the question as you read it, then your position in one sentence.
- 本論 1: your strongest reason, with one concrete example.
- 本論 2: the strongest objection, stated fairly, then your response.
- 結論: your position again, narrowed by what you conceded. No new material.

**Comparison (比較型):** 「AとBを比較し、あなたの考えを述べなさい」

- 序論: the one axis you'll compare on. Not everything, just one.
- 本論 1: A on that axis.
- 本論 2: B on the same axis, in the same order.
- 結論: which, under what conditions. The conditions are where the marks are.

**Data reading (資料型):** 「次のグラフを読み取り、考察しなさい」

- 読み取り: describe what the data shows. No interpretation yet.
- 考察 1: one plausible cause.
- 考察 2: an alternative cause, or a limit of the data.
- 結論: what can and can't be concluded from this figure alone.

Always save a few minutes at the end to read it once and fix grammar and 原稿用紙 rules.

The planner in [Study → Application → Essay](#/study/apply/essay) splits your time for each shape automatically, based on the time and length in your university's 募集要項, and counts characters as you write.
